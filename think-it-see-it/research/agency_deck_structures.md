# Agency Deck Structures — Research & Taxonomy

> Data foundation for THINK IT. SEE IT.
> Extracted from common patterns across top-tier advertising, branding, and strategy agencies.

---

## 1. UNIVERSAL DECK ANATOMY

Regardless of type, strong agency decks share a five-act backbone:

| Act | Purpose | Typical Scenes |
|-----|---------|----------------|
| **I. Frame** | Set context, establish credibility, define the problem space | Cover, Intro, Credentials, Brief Recap |
| **II. Diagnose** | Show understanding deeper than the client's own | Challenge, Audience, Market Context, Insight |
| **III. Unlock** | Reveal the strategic opportunity | Strategy, Proposition, Opportunity |
| **IV. Solve** | Present the creative answer | Route Reveal, Idea Board, Executions, Media Plan |
| **V. Land** | Make it real and actionable | Timeline, Budget, Production, Case Study, Closing |

## 2. DECK TYPE STRUCTURES

### 2.1 Credentials Deck

**Purpose:** Prove capability before a relationship begins.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Agency intro → Philosophy | Lead with belief, not history |
| Middle | Capabilities → Case studies (3–5) → Team | Each case study: challenge → idea → result |
| Close | Client list → Awards → Contact | Social proof, then invitation |

- **Narrative flow:** "Here's what we believe → Here's how we prove it → Here's what we could do together"
- **Strategic tension:** Appears implicitly — the tension between what agencies say and what they deliver
- **Visual intensity:** High. Portfolio-led. Image-heavy.
- **Proof level:** Heavy — case studies carry the deck
- **Creative routes:** Not applicable
- **Budget/timeline:** Not included

---

### 2.2 Pitch Deck

**Purpose:** Win new business. The highest-stakes deck type.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Brief recap → "What we heard" | Show you listened harder than competitors |
| Diagnosis | Challenge reframe → Audience deep-dive → Cultural/market context → Insight | The insight is the pivot point |
| Strategy | Strategic framework → Proposition → Positioning | Must feel inevitable, not imposed |
| Creative | Route reveal (2–3 routes) → Hero executions → Extension examples | Routes should feel genuinely different |
| Activation | Media plan → Channel strategy → Content ecosystem | Show how the idea lives |
| Landing | Timeline → Budget → Team → Next steps → Closing | End with momentum |

- **Narrative flow:** "We understood you better → We found something others missed → Here's the creative answer → Here's how it works → Let's go"
- **Strategic tension:** Explicit. Usually scene 4–6. The tension between where the brand is and where it could be.
- **Visual intensity:** Very high. Every scene designed.
- **Proof level:** Moderate — enough to ground the creative, not so much it kills momentum
- **Creative routes:** 2–3, presented after strategy, before activation
- **Budget/timeline:** Always present but positioned after the creative has landed

---

### 2.3 Strategy Deck

**Purpose:** Align stakeholders on direction before creative work begins.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Context → Objectives | Set the frame clearly |
| Analysis | Market landscape → Competitor audit → Audience segmentation → Cultural forces | Evidence-heavy |
| Synthesis | Key tensions → Core insight → Strategic territory | Where the thinking crystallizes |
| Direction | Strategic framework → Positioning → Messaging pillars → Guardrails | Must feel ownable |
| Next steps | Implications → Creative brief → Timeline | Bridge to the next phase |

- **Narrative flow:** "Here's the world → Here's what matters → Here's the opportunity → Here's the path"
- **Strategic tension:** Central. The entire deck builds toward identifying and resolving tensions.
- **Visual intensity:** Moderate. Clean, typography-led. Charts and frameworks dominate.
- **Proof level:** Very heavy — data, research, evidence throughout
- **Creative routes:** Not present. Strategy decks define the space; creative decks fill it.
- **Budget/timeline:** Light — usually just "next steps" timing

---

### 2.4 Campaign Deck

**Purpose:** Present a specific campaign idea for approval.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Brief recap → Objective | Quick context |
| Strategic foundation | Audience → Insight → Proposition | Compact — strategy already agreed |
| Campaign idea | Campaign name → Manifesto → Hero execution | The big reveal moment |
| Executions | Channel executions (5–8) → Content examples → Social examples | Show the idea's range |
| Activation | Media plan → Phasing → KPIs | Make it measurable |
| Production | Timeline → Budget → Production approach | Make it buildable |

- **Narrative flow:** "Remember the brief → Here's the insight → Here's the idea → Here's how it lives everywhere → Here's how we make it"
- **Strategic tension:** Brief — appears in insight scene, then resolved immediately by the idea
- **Visual intensity:** Maximum. Campaign decks are the most visual of all types.
- **Proof level:** Light — just enough to ground the insight
- **Creative routes:** Usually 1 (already selected). Sometimes 2 for client choice.
- **Budget/timeline:** Always present. Often detailed.

---

### 2.5 Innovation Deck

**Purpose:** Propose new products, services, or ventures.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Provocation → Opportunity space | Start with a "what if" |
| Context | Market forces → Technology shifts → Consumer behaviour change | Build the case for change |
| Concept | Innovation concept → Value proposition → User journey | The new thing |
| Validation | Prototype → User feedback → Market sizing | Prove it's viable |
| Roadmap | Phases → Investment → Team → Go/no-go criteria | Make the decision easy |

- **Narrative flow:** "The world is changing → Here's the gap → Here's our answer → Here's the proof → Here's the plan"
- **Strategic tension:** Between the current state and the future state
- **Visual intensity:** High. Prototypes and mockups dominate.
- **Proof level:** Heavy on market evidence and user validation
- **Creative routes:** Usually 1 concept, sometimes 2–3 concept directions
- **Budget/timeline:** Central — investment framing is core to innovation decks

---

### 2.6 Workshop Deck

**Purpose:** Facilitate collaborative thinking, not present a finished answer.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Objectives → Agenda → Rules of play | Set expectations for participation |
| Stimulus | Provocations → Case studies → Inspiration → Data points | Fuel the discussion |
| Exercises | Exercise briefs → Templates → Breakout instructions | Structured activities |
| Synthesis | Capture templates → Voting frameworks → Prioritization | Harvest the outputs |
| Close | Summary → Next steps → Follow-up | Bridge to action |

- **Narrative flow:** "Here's why we're here → Here's what we know → Let's think together → Let's decide → Let's act"
- **Strategic tension:** Introduced as stimulus, not resolved — the workshop resolves it
- **Visual intensity:** Moderate. Clean, legible, lots of white space for projection.
- **Proof level:** Selective — enough to provoke, not enough to prescribe
- **Creative routes:** Not present. Workshops generate routes, not present them.
- **Budget/timeline:** Only in "next steps"

---

### 2.7 Case Study Deck

**Purpose:** Tell the story of a successful project.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Cover → Client context → Challenge | Set the scene |
| Solution | Insight → Strategy → Creative idea | How we got there |
| Execution | Campaign executions → Channel examples → Content | What it looked like |
| Results | Metrics → Awards → Client testimony → Cultural impact | The payoff |
| Close | Learnings → Implications | What it means for the next project |

- **Narrative flow:** "There was a problem → We found the truth → We made this → It worked → Here's what we learned"
- **Strategic tension:** In the challenge scene — then fully resolved by results
- **Visual intensity:** Very high. Case studies live on visuals.
- **Proof level:** Maximum — results are the point
- **Creative routes:** Historical — shows the route that was chosen
- **Budget/timeline:** Rarely included

---

### 2.8 Keynote-Style Deck

**Purpose:** Inspire an audience. Thought leadership.

| Section | Scenes | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Opening | Title → Provocative question or statistic | Hook immediately |
| Argument | Thesis → Evidence → Counterpoint → Synthesis | Build a compelling argument |
| Examples | Case studies → Cultural examples → Data visualizations | Make it concrete |
| Implication | "So what?" → Call to action → Closing thought | Land with resonance |

- **Narrative flow:** "Here's something you didn't expect → Here's why it matters → Here's what to do about it"
- **Strategic tension:** The entire deck IS the tension — between conventional wisdom and a new perspective
- **Visual intensity:** Very high. Single-idea-per-scene. Cinematic.
- **Proof level:** Selective — curated evidence, not exhaustive
- **Creative routes:** Not applicable
- **Budget/timeline:** Never included

## 3. CROSS-CUTTING PATTERNS

### 3.1 Opening Patterns (First 3 Scenes)

| Pattern | Description | Best For |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **The Reframe** | "You think the problem is X. It's actually Y." | Pitch, Strategy |
| **The Provocation** | Bold statement or statistic to create tension | Keynote, Innovation |
| **The Empathy Open** | "We heard you. Here's what we understood." | Pitch, Campaign |
| **The Context Set** | Neutral landscape → zoom to opportunity | Strategy, Innovation |
| **The Portfolio Lead** | Show best work first, earn the right to speak | Credentials |
| **The Rules of Play** | Set expectations for participation | Workshop |

### 3.2 Where Creative Routes Appear

| Deck Type | Route Position | Number of Routes |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pitch | After strategy, before activation (scenes 8–12 of ~20) | 2–3 |
| Campaign | After brief recap (scenes 4–6 of ~15) | 1–2 |
| Innovation | After market context (scenes 5–7 of ~15) | 1–3 |
| Strategy | Not present | 0 |
| Workshop | Not present (generated during workshop) | 0 |
| Credentials | Not present | 0 |
| Case Study | Retrospective (shows chosen route) | 1 |
| Keynote | Not applicable | 0 |

### 3.3 Budget/Timeline Positioning

**Universal rule:** Budget and timeline ALWAYS come AFTER the creative idea has been presented and the audience is emotionally invested. Presenting budget before the idea kills momentum.

Exception: Workshop decks, where next-steps timing is at the very end.

### 3.4 Proof and Evidence Distribution

| Deck Type | Evidence Density | Primary Evidence Types |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Strategy | Very high (60%+ of scenes) | Data, research, competitor analysis |
| Pitch | Moderate (30–40%) | Insights, cultural references, selective data |
| Campaign | Light (15–20%) | Consumer insight, brief reference |
| Innovation | High (40–50%) | Market data, user research, prototypes |
| Credentials | High (50%+) | Case studies, results, awards |
| Workshop | Selective | Provocations, stimulus, curated data |
| Case Study | Maximum (70%+) | Results, metrics, testimonials |
| Keynote | Curated (25–35%) | Stories, statistics, cultural examples |

## 4. STRUCTURAL ANTI-PATTERNS (What Bad Decks Do)

| Anti-Pattern | Description | Why It Fails |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| **The Data Dump** | 40 slides of research before any point | Audience loses patience before the payoff |
| **The Logo Wall** | Leading with client logos instead of thinking | Commoditizes the agency |
| **The Single Route** | Only one creative direction with no contrast | Client can't choose; feels forced |
| **Budget Up Front** | Showing cost before showing value | Creates anchoring bias against the work |
| **The Template Deck** | Identical structure regardless of brief | Signals lack of strategic thinking |
| **No Tension** | Straight to solution without diagnosing the problem | Idea feels rootless |
| **The 80-Slide Monster** | Every thought included, nothing edited | Respect the audience's time |
| **Execution Without Strategy** | Pretty pictures with no strategic logic | Work feels arbitrary |

---

## 5. IMPLICATIONS FOR THINK IT. SEE IT.

1. **The system must know deck types** — different briefs need different structures
2. **Scene sequencing is not random** — it follows proven narrative patterns
3. **Creative routes must be positioned correctly** — after strategy, before activation
4. **Budget/timeline must come last** — always after emotional buy-in
5. **Proof density varies by type** — the system should adjust evidence scenes accordingly
6. **Opening patterns matter enormously** — the first 3 scenes determine engagement
7. **Anti-patterns should be actively avoided** — the system should warn users
