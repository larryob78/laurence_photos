# Quality Rubric — Deck Output Evaluation Framework

> Scoring system for evaluating the quality of decks generated by THINK IT. SEE IT.
> Used for: automated quality checks, human QA, AI fine-tuning evaluation, user feedback alignment.

---

## SCORING SYSTEM

Each dimension is scored **1–5**:

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Poor | Fundamentally broken. Would embarrass the presenter. |
| 2 | Weak | Has the right shape but significant gaps. Needs major rework. |
| 3 | Acceptable | Functional and competent. Would pass but wouldn't impress. |
| 4 | Strong | Professional quality. Minor refinements only. |
| 5 | Exceptional | Would win the room. Agency-quality output. |

**Minimum viable quality for product output: 3 across all dimensions.**
**Target quality: 4+ on at least 7 of 10 dimensions.**

---

## DIMENSION 1: CLARITY

> Can the audience understand the message without effort?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Confused structure. Unclear what the deck is about. Scenes don't connect. |
| 2 | General direction is visible but key messages are buried. Too much text. Jargon unexplained. |
| 3 | Core message is clear. Scene sequence makes sense. Some scenes could be tighter. |
| 4 | Every scene has a clear purpose. Headlines communicate the point without needing body text. Tight editing. |
| 5 | Crystal clear. Each scene advances exactly one idea. A new reader could present this cold. |

**Good looks like:** Single idea per scene. Headlines that work as standalone statements. No scene where you wonder "what's the point of this?"

**Common failure modes:**
- Multiple competing ideas on one scene
- Headlines that describe instead of assert ("About Our Audience" vs. "They Don't Trust You Yet")
- Body text that contradicts or dilutes the headline
- Scenes with no clear role in the narrative

---

## DIMENSION 2: STRATEGIC COHERENCE

> Does the deck tell a logically sound strategic story?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | No visible strategy. Random scenes. Creative without foundation. |
| 2 | Strategy is present but disconnected from creative. Insight doesn't lead to idea. |
| 3 | Clear strategic thread. Insight → Opportunity → Idea is traceable. Some gaps in logic. |
| 4 | Tight strategic narrative. Each scene builds on the previous. The idea feels earned. |
| 5 | Airtight. A sceptical CMO would struggle to find a logical gap. Strategy and creative are inseparable. |

**Good looks like:** You can trace a clear line from challenge → insight → strategy → creative idea. Nothing feels arbitrary.

**Common failure modes:**
- Insight that doesn't connect to the creative idea
- Strategy section that feels like a separate document bolted on
- Creative routes that could work for any brand (not specific enough)
- Missing the "so what?" — why this strategy for THIS brand right now

---

## DIMENSION 3: PERSUASIVE STRENGTH

> Would this deck change someone's mind or get them to say yes?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | No persuasive structure. Informational but not compelling. |
| 2 | Some persuasive elements but weak sequencing. The "ask" comes too early or is unclear. |
| 3 | Competent persuasion. Clear ask. Reasonable evidence. Would work with a warm audience. |
| 4 | Strong persuasion. Creates tension, resolves it compellingly. Evidence placed at key moments. Hard to say no. |
| 5 | Masterful. Uses narrative tension, social proof, scarcity, and emotional resonance. The audience feels they discovered the answer themselves. |

**Good looks like:** The deck creates a feeling of inevitability — by the time you reach the idea, it feels like the only possible answer.

**Common failure modes:**
- Asking for commitment before building the case
- All logic, no emotion (or vice versa)
- Weak closing — the deck fizzles instead of lands
- No tension — if there's no problem, there's no need for a solution

---

## DIMENSION 4: ORIGINALITY

> Does this feel fresh, or does it feel like a template?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Generic. Could be any brand, any agency, any brief. |
| 2 | Some specificity but mostly category clichés. "Innovation" decks that look like every other one. |
| 3 | Tailored to the brief. Not remarkable but not generic. Competent craft. |
| 4 | Genuinely interesting. Unexpected angles, surprising structures, memorable language. |
| 5 | Would make other agencies jealous. Original thinking expressed through original creative. Feels like it could only be for this brand. |

**Good looks like:** Headlines you haven't read before. A creative route you couldn't predict from the brief. Visual directions that feel ownable.

**Common failure modes:**
- Category clichés ("In a world where...", "Reimagining the future of...")
- Creative routes that are just tone variations of the same idea
- Visual worlds described with generic mood board language ("clean and modern")
- Propositions so broad they could be for any brand

---

## DIMENSION 5: VISUAL SOPHISTICATION

> Does the visual design support and elevate the content?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Ugly or broken. Bad typography, clashing colours, cluttered layouts. |
| 2 | Functional but uninspiring. Default template feeling. Inconsistent styling. |
| 3 | Clean and professional. Consistent system. Nothing offensive but nothing exciting. |
| 4 | Distinctive visual identity. Typography, colour, and layout work together. Feels designed, not templated. |
| 5 | Award-worthy visual craft. Every scene is a considered composition. Visual system is as compelling as the content. |

**Good looks like:** A consistent visual language across all scenes. Type hierarchy that guides the eye. Colour used with intention. White space used with confidence.

**Common failure modes:**
- Too many fonts/colours (visual noise)
- Inconsistent spacing and alignment
- Colour choices that fight readability
- Layouts that don't adapt to content density

---

## DIMENSION 6: BRAND CONSISTENCY

> Does the output respect and express the brand correctly?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Off-brand. Wrong colours, wrong tone, wrong everything. |
| 2 | Partially on-brand but significant deviations. Tone mismatch or colour inconsistency. |
| 3 | Generally on-brand. Colours and typography correct. Tone approximately right. |
| 4 | Strongly on-brand. Visual system, tone, and vocabulary all aligned. Feels like the brand made it. |
| 5 | Extends the brand beautifully. Not just compliant but creative within the brand system. Feels like the best version of the brand. |

**Good looks like:** If you covered the logo, you'd still know which brand this was. The deck feels native to the brand, not applied.

**Common failure modes:**
- Using brand colours but not in the right proportions
- Headlines that sound like the agency, not the brand
- Ignoring brand typography rules
- Visual style that contradicts brand personality

*Note: Only scoreable when a BrandKit is loaded. Without BrandKit, score N/A.*

---

## DIMENSION 7: EDITABILITY

> Can the user easily modify and improve the output?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Monolithic. Unclear structure. Editing one thing breaks other things. |
| 2 | Editable but painful. Unclear which fields matter. No scene-level editing. |
| 3 | Scene-level editing works. Fields are clearly labelled. Changes save correctly. |
| 4 | Intuitive editing. Each field has a clear purpose. Scenes can be reordered, added, removed. |
| 5 | Delightful editing experience. Inline editing, live preview, undo/redo, AI-assisted refinement. Feels like writing in a premium tool. |

**Good looks like:** A user can change a headline and see the result instantly. Adding a new scene feels natural. The structure remains coherent after edits.

**Common failure modes:**
- Editing a headline that's too tightly coupled to body text
- Reordering scenes that breaks the narrative flow
- No way to regenerate individual scenes
- Edits that don't persist

---

## DIMENSION 8: MEDIA RICHNESS

> Does the deck make appropriate use of visual and media elements?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Text only. No visual elements. Looks like a document, not a presentation. |
| 2 | Some visual elements but feel decorative, not purposeful. Stock photography that says nothing. |
| 3 | Appropriate media. Visuals support the content. Art direction notes are useful. |
| 4 | Rich and purposeful media. Visual direction is specific and evocative. Each image/video adds meaning. |
| 5 | Cinematic. Media and content are inseparable. Visual direction could brief a photographer or art director directly. |

**Good looks like:** Visual direction notes that are specific enough to act on ("Overhead shot of a crowded market at golden hour, shallow depth of field" not "nice photo of people").

**Common failure modes:**
- Generic visual direction ("Modern and clean")
- No visual direction at all (text-only scenes where images would help)
- Visual suggestions that don't match the brand or tone
- Same visual treatment for every scene regardless of content

---

## DIMENSION 9: AUDIENCE FIT

> Is the deck calibrated for its intended audience?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Audience completely misread. Wrong level of detail, wrong tone, wrong references. |
| 2 | Generally aimed at the right people but miscalibrated. Too technical for non-experts, or too basic for specialists. |
| 3 | Appropriate for the audience. Right level of detail. Tone is suitable. |
| 4 | Well-calibrated. Language, evidence, and tone match the audience's expectations and knowledge level. |
| 5 | Perfectly tuned. Feels like it was written by someone who deeply understands this audience. References, proof level, and vocabulary are exactly right. |

**Good looks like:** A pitch to a CMO uses different language than a workshop with junior creatives. The deck adjusts its evidence density, vocabulary, and tone to match who's in the room.

**Common failure modes:**
- Using agency jargon with non-agency clients
- Oversimplifying for a sophisticated audience
- Wrong proof level (too much data for a creative audience, too little for an analytical one)
- Tone that doesn't match the audience's culture

---

## DIMENSION 10: PRODUCTION READINESS

> Could this deck be presented or produced without significant additional work?

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Needs complete rework. Missing scenes, broken structure, unusable as-is. |
| 2 | Skeleton is there but every scene needs significant work. Would take hours to make presentable. |
| 3 | Could be presented with 30–60 minutes of editing. Most scenes are usable. Some need revision. |
| 4 | Near-presentable. 15–30 minutes of polish. Headlines land, structure works, visual direction is clear. |
| 5 | Ready to present. A skilled presenter could walk into a room with this deck and deliver a compelling presentation immediately. |

**Good looks like:** Every scene has a purpose. No placeholder text. Speaker notes are useful. Visual direction is actionable. The deck respects standard presentation timing (2–3 minutes per scene).

**Common failure modes:**
- Placeholder content that wasn't replaced
- Scenes without speaker notes
- Deck that's too long (30+ scenes for a 45-minute meeting)
- Missing scenes (no closing, no timeline, no ask)

---

## COMPOSITE SCORING

### Overall Quality Score
**Weighted average** (not equal — some dimensions matter more):

| Dimension | Weight | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Clarity | 15% | Non-negotiable. Confusion kills everything. |
| Strategic Coherence | 15% | The core value proposition of the product. |
| Persuasive Strength | 12% | Why decks exist. |
| Originality | 8% | Important but secondary to clarity and strategy. |
| Visual Sophistication | 10% | Expected at this level. |
| Brand Consistency | 10% | Critical when BrandKit is present. |
| Editability | 10% | Core to the "living" proposition. |
| Media Richness | 5% | Nice to have at MVP. |
| Audience Fit | 10% | Drives real-world effectiveness. |
| Production Readiness | 5% | The final test. |

### Quality Grades

| Grade | Score Range | Meaning |
|-------|------------|---------|
| A | 4.5–5.0 | Exceptional. Ship it. |
| B | 3.5–4.4 | Strong. Minor polish needed. |
| C | 2.5–3.4 | Acceptable. Needs editing. |
| D | 1.5–2.4 | Weak. Major revision needed. |
| F | 1.0–1.4 | Failed. Regenerate. |
